\subsection{Separating acquisition representation from a deployed predictor}
The executed OCM follow-up keeps the predictor and acquired support responses separate from the feature view used to choose those responses. F0 uses six operating descriptors; F1 additionally uses the inherited role-aware composition representation. Operating V and composition V choose nested support orders from the two corresponding acquisition views, then the same acquired rows are passed to both predictors. The acquisition regularization schedule is the frozen F1 schedule for both views. This crossing tests whether chemistry useful for deciding where to measure must also be consumed by the deployed response model. It is an offline, finite-pool design problem with known query inputs; query responses are excluded from acquisition.

We add two established design controls: kernel V minimizes mean working posterior variance using an RBF kernel, with $\gamma\in\{0.1,1,10\}$ chosen on historical pseudo-sources; robust multi-view V minimizes the maximum fraction of remaining working variance across operating and composition views. Both use finite greedy selection with the inherited budget schedule. These are implementations of standard kernel and robust-design ideas, rather than claimed inventions or exact reproductions of a named recent algorithm. Eleven acquisition policies, including random, coverage, fixed-view, learned-view and correspondence-shuffle controls, are crossed with twelve predictors in each of two prediction representations. Original finite grids also tune local ridge, local RBF and a blend separately for each policy on historical sources only.

A candidate view gate predicts the actual deployed F0 blend's five-label loss difference, $\delta=L_C-L_O$, from eight response-free descriptors of pool/query size, operating and chemistry variation, chemistry novelty, operating--chemistry residual variation, chemistry mean shift and the disagreement between the two five-point orders. A ridge meta-regressor uses fixed penalty 10. It selects operating acquisition only if $\widehat\delta>q$, otherwise retaining composition acquisition; $q$ is the empirical 80th percentile of mean absolute leave-group-out meta errors. This is an empirical conservative margin, not a conformal guarantee. New gate and kernel choices exclude the historical pseudo-source's leakage group; inherited adapter settings remain conditional on their earlier outer-history selection. The complete twenty-five seed/fold comparisons reuse the verified archived outer bases and rebuild matching historical source-disjoint geometry. Thus this follow-up adds no outer-backbone refits to the historical comparison; it adds actual gate/control fitting, acquisition and prediction comparisons. Historical outcomes had informed the research question, so this is method development.

The locked historical primary family compares the conservative gate with composition V, operating V, kernel V, robust multi-view V and a fixed historical best view, using frozen F0 blending, random query and five labels. Draws are averaged within seed, then seeds within source. Equal-source loss differences receive twenty thousand leakage-group bootstrap resamples and 99\% percentile intervals for the five-comparison family, conditional on fitted components. Other predictor, budget, stress and mechanism contrasts are exploratory. The new random support seed includes the outer seed; its mean can differ slightly from the earlier five-panel random mean, whereas matched deterministic composition/operating values reproduce that geometry analysis.

\subsection{A frozen application to new author-reported measurements}
After the complete historical analysis and audit, fifty-six backbone fits produce a final historical pipeline: two representations, nine candidates in three historical group folds, and two full-history models. Local adapters, gate and acquisition parameters are selected from historical out-of-fold responses. Code, model, input and parameter hashes are frozen before new-cohort download and scoring. We then curate the publicly released measurements of Nishimura et al.\ \cite{nishimura2023new}. The author CADS CSV has 2928 rows. Only its explicitly identified SVR-1, SVR-2 and three Bayesian validation phases enter; mixed literature/HTS training inputs, predicted lists, controls and unresolved unpublished blocks are excluded. Removing 1024 such rows, 120 rows without a complete temperature/response record, and 45 repeated catalyst-condition occurrences leaves 1739 measured rows for 116 catalysts. First occurrences are retained from original CSV order, without averaging labels or inspecting prediction errors.

The reported feed ratio and Celsius temperature are mapped to the historical schema, retaining the original composition and support formulas in row provenance. Single-cation oxide supports are mapped to their support-cation tokens to match that schema. Total and partial pressures, contact time and mol percentages are not inferred; historical-only preprocessing handles these missing fields. The carbon-basis interpretation of C$_2$ yield is checked against ethane/ethylene yield sums and the reported methane conversion/carbon balance. Bibliographic and rounded-record screening against the historical and previously evaluated external inputs is retained, including three low-yield rounded-condition coincidences with 2011 records that do not identify copied runs. The new paper explicitly describes the original validation measurements. This supports a new publication-measurement application, not certified separation from every historical laboratory or catalyst batch.

All five validation phases count as one source and one experiment family. The frozen pipeline evaluates all eleven policies and twelve predictors, with the unchanged nested budgets and response-blind partitioning. This is a retrospective application to published measurements, not a prospective experiment performed by this study. With one family, we report point estimates and withhold a population confidence interval. The cohort has been evaluated once and cannot serve again as an unseen confirmation set for a subsequently redesigned method.
